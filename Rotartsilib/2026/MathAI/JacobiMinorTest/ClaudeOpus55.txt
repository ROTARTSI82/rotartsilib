  The sign: without it the identity is false. Take n = 2, I = {1}, J = {2}. Then
  A[I,J] = a₁₂, but adj(A)[{1},{2}] = −a₁₂. The correct form of Jacobi's
  theorem is

    det(A) · adj(A)[Jᶜ, Iᶜ] = (−1)^(ΣI + ΣJ) · det(A)^(n−k) · A[I, J]

  The sign is the same whether you index from 0 (Fin n) or from 1, so the
  0-indexed version in Lean is fine. It holds over any commutative ring, and A
  doesn't need to be invertible.

  Main theorem: DetPoly.det_mul_det_submatrix_adjugate. The minor A[I, J] is
  (A.submatrix (I.orderEmbOfFin hI) (J.orderEmbOfFin hJ)).det, with hI : #I = k
  and hJ : #J = k.

  How the proof works (outlined in the file's header docstring):
  1. Reorder A's rows as I then Iᶜ, and its columns as J then Jᶜ. Call the
     result B, and reorder adj(A) the opposite way to get C, so that B·C =
     det(A)·1. Splitting B·C into blocks gives B₁₁C₁₂ + B₁₂C₂₂ = 0 and B₂₁C₁₂ + 
     B₂₂C₂₂ = det A • 1.
  2. Let M = fromBlocks 1 C₁₂ 0 C₂₂. Then B·M = fromBlocks B₁₁ 0 B₂₁ (det A • 
     1), which is block-triangular. Taking determinants gives det B · det C₂₂ =
     det B₁₁ · det(A)^(n−k).
  3. det B = sign(σ) · det A for a permutation σ, and linear_combination (using
     sign² = 1) finishes.

  Helper lemmas:
  - sign_shuffle: Mathlib has no lemma for the sign of a "shuffle" permutation
    (one that lists I in order, then Iᶜ in order), so I proved it. It starts
    from Mathlib's Perm.sign_eq_prod_prod_Iio and counts the inverted pairs.
  - sign_eq_prod_ite: writes the sign of a permutation as a product over its
    inverted pairs; sign_shuffle uses it.
  - finSumComplEquiv I hI : Fin k ⊕ Fin (n - k) ≃ Fin n: the "I then Iᶜ"
    enumeration.
  - det_submatrix_equiv: det(A.submatrix e f) = sign(f⁻¹ ∘ e) · det A.

  Mathlib lemmas it uses: fromBlocks_toBlocks, fromBlocks_multiply,
  fromBlocks_inj, det_fromBlocks_zero₂₁/₁₂, det_permute,
  det_submatrix_equiv_self, mul_adjugate and Finset.orderEmbOfFin.
