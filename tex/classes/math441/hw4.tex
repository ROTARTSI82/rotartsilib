\paragraph{Problem 2.} $\mathbb R$ with the co-finite or co-countable topology is not Hausdorff.

\begin{proof}
    By the definition of Hausdorff, for any two distinct points we require two disjoint, nonempty open sets. However, we can show that in both of these topologies, all nonempty open sets have nonempty intersection. All open sets in the co-finite topology are also open in the co-countable topology, as finite sets are countable. Thus, it suffices to show this result for nonempty open sets in the co-countable topology.

    Let $U_1$, $U_2$ be nonempty open sets, and let $F_1 = U_1^C$ and $F_2 = U_2^C$. Note that since $F_1 \neq \mathbb R$ and $F_2 \neq \mathbb R$, both $F_1$ and $F_2$ are countable. Assume for contradiction that $U_1 \cap U_2 = \varnothing$. Then, we can compute $\varnothing = F_1^C \cap F_2^C = (\mathbb R \setminus F_1) \cap (\mathbb R \setminus F_2) = \mathbb R \setminus (F_1 \cup F_2)$. This would imply that $F_1 \cup F_2 \supseteq \mathbb R$, but note that $F_1 \cup F_2$ is a countable set while $\mathbb R$ is uncountable. Thus, we have a contradiction, as every subset of a countable set is countable.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Problem 3.} Let $f, g : X \to Y$ be continuous maps, and let $Y$ be Hausdorff. Then the following set is closed:
\[W = \{ x \in X : f(x) = g(x) \}. \]

\begin{proof}
    Let $x \in W^C$, and since $Y$ is Hausdorff and $f(x) \neq g(x)$, choose disjoint open sets $V_1, V_2 \subseteq Y$ such that $V_1 \ni f(x)$ and $V_2 \ni g(x)$. Consider $U = f^{-1}(V_1) \cap g^{-1}(V_2)$, and note that $U\subseteq X$ is open by the continuity of $f$ and $g$ and $x \in U$. Also note that for any $p \in U$, $f(p) \neq g(p)$ since $f(p) \in V_1$ while $g(p) \in V_2$ and $V_1 \cap V_2 = \varnothing$. Thus, $U \subseteq W^C$.

    We have shown that for all $x \in W^C$, there exists an open set $U \ni x$ such that $U \subseteq W^C$. Thus, $W^C$ can be expressed as a union of open sets, so $W$ is closed as desired.
\end{proof}
